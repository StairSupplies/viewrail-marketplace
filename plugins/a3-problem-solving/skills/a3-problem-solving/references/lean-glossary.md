# Lean Glossary (for the A3 skill)

Compact reference the skill loads on demand to clarify lean terms during coaching.

## Core A3 vocabulary

- **A3** , a one-page structured problem-solving report on landscape A3 paper. Named for the paper size. Forces concision and visual clarity.
- **Problem-Solving A3** , the variant covered by this skill, used when a process is broken and needs root-cause work. (Other variants exist: Strategy A3, Status A3.)
- **PDCA** , Plan, Do, Check, Act. The cycle the A3 itself enacts: §1 to §4 are Plan; §5 is Do; §6 is Check; §7 is Act.

## Diagnosis tools

- **5 Whys** , iterative interrogation to trace symptom to root cause. Each "Why" must be verifiable with data or direct observation, not speculation. Stop when the next Why would be a system or process you can change.
- **Fishbone (Ishikawa)** , alternative to 5 Whys, organizes possible causes by category (Man, Machine, Method, Material, Measurement, Environment). Useful when more than one root cause is suspected.
- **Gemba** , the actual place where work happens. "Go to the gemba" means observe directly, don't theorize from a desk.

## Cause concepts

- **Symptom** , what you see (late shipments, defects, complaints).
- **Root cause** , what, if removed, would prevent the symptom from recurring. The "if I fix this, would the problem stop coming back?" test.
- **System cause vs person cause** , a system cause is upstream (a missing checklist, an unclear handoff). A person cause is downstream ("Bob forgot"). The A3 lens favors system causes because they are durable; replacing Bob doesn't fix a missing checklist.

## Action concepts

- **Countermeasure** , an action that addresses a root cause (not a symptom). Each countermeasure on an A3 must trace back to a specific named root cause from §4.
- **Standardize** , the act of locking a successful countermeasure into the standard work (SOPs, checklists, training) so the gain holds.
- **Kaizen** , small continuous improvements; the philosophy A3 work runs on.

## Flow concepts (relevant for §1, Background)

- **Constraint** , the step in the flow that limits throughput. A3 problems often surface as constraints in someone else's downstream step.
- **Flow** , the end-to-end path of a unit of work (an order, a project, a customer interaction).
- **Takt time, kanban, value stream** , available on request if the A3 touches manufacturing flow.
