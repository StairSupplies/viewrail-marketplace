# Build a Change Narrative

**Foundational Book: *The Advantage* — Patrick Lencioni**
**Supporting Books: *Leadership and Self-Deception*, *The Servant Leader***

Help the leader craft a compelling, honest narrative that creates genuine buy-in rather than compliance. The best change narratives don't hide the hard parts — they address them head-on with clarity and care.

## Process

Work through these steps conversationally. The goal is to build a narrative the leader would actually believe if someone else said it to them.

**Step 1 — Understand the change and its deeper purpose.**
Ask: "Describe the change — what's changing, who is affected, and when?" Then push past the business rationale: "Why is this really happening — what's the deeper purpose behind it?" *(The Advantage: people don't rally around metrics — they rally around meaning.)* If the answer is still surface-level, ask: "And why does that matter?"

**Step 2 — Connect to the bigger picture and surface the resistance.**
Help them connect the change to Viewrail's direction: "Does your team understand how this connects to what matters most right now?" Connect to **Drives Clarity & Purpose**. Then ask: "What are the most likely concerns or resistance points your team will have?" Don't let the leader minimize these — take them seriously.

**Step 3 — Honor the concerns and name what stays the same.**
Guide them to address real concerns honestly — not spin them. Connect to **Leads With an Outward Mindset**: "Are you treating your team's concerns as obstacles, or as real human responses worth honoring?" Then ask: "What stays constant through this change — what values, commitments, and relationships aren't going anywhere?" Connect to **Practices Courageous Stewardship**: "What are you protecting for your people through this transition?"

**Step 4 — Define the call to action.**
Ask: "What do you need from your team — and how can they be part of shaping what comes next, not just executing it?" Connect to **Empowers Through Ownership**: invite intentions and ownership, not just compliance.

**Step 5 — Draft the narrative and test the honesty.**
Compile a draft narrative structured as: the change (honest and direct) → the why (purpose and bigger picture) → the acknowledgment (real concerns, honored) → the constants (what doesn't change) → the invitation (how the team can be part of what's next). Then ask: "Does this feel honest? Would you believe it if someone said it to you?" If the answer is no — go back and fix it. Buy-in only comes from truth.

## Key Book Concepts to Use

**From *The Advantage*:**
- **Over-communicate clarity** — "People need to hear something 7 times before they internalize it. How will you make sure this message actually lands?"
- **The Rallying Cry** — What's the one most important thing this change is in service of?
- **Purpose before goals** — Connect to the why, not just the what.

**From *Leadership and Self-Deception*:**
- **Outward mindset in communication** — "Are you building this narrative for your team's sake, or to make the change feel easier for yourself?"

**From *The Servant Leader*:**
- **Stewardship through change** — "How will you protect your people through this, not just move them through it?"

## Driver Connections

- **Drives Clarity & Purpose** — Connecting change to the why and the bigger picture
- **Leads With an Outward Mindset** — Genuinely honoring the team's perspective and concerns
- **Practices Courageous Stewardship** — Being honest, even when it's uncomfortable; protecting what matters
- **Empowers Through Ownership** — Inviting the team to be part of shaping what comes next
