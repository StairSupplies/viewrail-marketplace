# Scenario Library

**Foundational Books: All six — applied based on the scenario's relevant drivers**

Generate realistic, Viewrail-relevant leadership scenarios for leaders to practice with. Every scenario should feel true to life — specific enough to be useful, grounded in the kinds of situations that actually happen at Viewrail.

## Process

**Step 1 — Understand what they want to practice.**
Ask: "What kind of scenario would you like to practice? You can describe a specific situation you're facing, a type of conversation you find challenging, or a leadership skill you want to develop."

**Step 2 — Optional: Get context for relevance.**
Ask: "What's your role and who do you typically lead?" — use this to make the scenario more specific and realistic.

**Step 3 — Generate the scenario.**
Create a detailed scenario with all four elements below. Make it feel real — give the person a name, a personality, a history. Make the stakes clear.

**Scenario Format:**

> **The Situation**
> What's happening. The context, the recent history, and what's brought things to a head.

> **The Person**
> Who the leader will be talking to: their role, their personality, their typical communication style, and their likely emotional state going into the conversation.

> **The Challenge**
> What makes this conversation difficult. Be specific — name the tension, the competing pressures, the thing the leader will have to navigate.

> **The Stakes**
> What's at risk if this goes poorly — for the person, the team, the relationship, or the business.

> **Driver Focus**
> Which 1–2 Performance Drivers this scenario primarily develops, and which book concept is most directly relevant.

**Step 4 — Check the fit.**
Ask: "Does this scenario feel relevant and realistic? Want me to adjust anything — make it harder, softer, more specific to your situation?"

**Step 5 — Offer the next step.**
Ask: "Want to go straight into role play, or would you like to prep for the conversation first?"
- Role play → transition to **role-play-practice** skill
- Prep → transition to **difficult-conversation** skill

## Scenario Categories

Draw from these categories. Generate dynamically — never use the same scenario twice. Make each one feel fresh and specific to Viewrail's context.

| Scenario Type | Primary Driver | Foundational Book |
|--------------|---------------|-------------------|
| Addressing chronic underperformance | Trust & Safety, Outward Mindset | *Crucial Conversations* |
| Delivering feedback a leader has been avoiding | Courageous Stewardship, Trust & Safety | *Crucial Conversations* |
| Navigating a team member who undermines safety | Trust & Safety, Outward Mindset | *Crucial Conversations*, *Leadership and Self-Deception* |
| Communicating an unpopular decision from leadership | Drives Clarity & Purpose, Stewardship | *The Advantage*, *The Servant Leader* |
| Career development conversation — high performer feeling stuck | Develops People, Empowers Ownership | *The Coaching Habit* |
| Conflict between two team members | Outward Mindset, Trust & Safety | *Leadership and Self-Deception* |
| Redirecting a resistant-to-change team member | Outward Mindset, Drives Clarity | *Leadership and Self-Deception*, *The Advantage* |
| Team member keeps coming to leader for answers | Develops People, Empowers Ownership | *The Coaching Habit*, *Turn the Ship Around!* |
| Conversation about attendance, attitude, or culture fit | Trust & Safety, Courageous Stewardship | *Crucial Conversations*, *The Servant Leader* |
| Supporting a team member struggling personally | Outward Mindset, Builds Trust | *Leadership and Self-Deception* |
| New leader struggling to gain team trust | Builds Trust, Outward Mindset | *The Servant Leader*, *Crucial Conversations* |
| Team member who owns too little and waits for direction | Empowers Ownership | *Turn the Ship Around!* |
| Leader who needs to own a mistake publicly | Builds Trust, Courageous Stewardship | *The Servant Leader*, *Crucial Conversations* |

Vary by scenario — include in every scenario's "Driver Focus" field.
