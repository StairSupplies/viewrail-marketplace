# Coaching Conversation Flow — Viewrail Leadership Reference

*Viewrail leadership development content — complete conversation outline for coaching conversations*

A structured coaching conversation has six phases: Preparation, Opening, Exploration, Creating the Path Forward, Social Contracting, and Closing. Each phase has a specific purpose and a set of moves that keep the leader in coach mode rather than advice-giving mode.

Use this reference when a leader is preparing a specific coaching conversation — especially when they need a concrete arc to follow, not just isolated questions.

---

## Pre-Conversation Preparation

Before entering the conversation, the leader should reflect on three questions:
- What specific behavior, performance issue, or development opportunity are you addressing?
- What outcome do you hope to achieve by the end of this conversation?
- What does success look like for this employee after this coaching?

**Coaching move:** Ask the leader these questions directly before they start preparing questions. Their answers reveal whether they're entering coach mode or evaluator mode.

---

## Opening: Framing the Conversation (Coaching Style)

**Goal:** Position as a thinking partner, not an evaluator.

**Openers that work:**
- "I'd like to have a conversation about [topic] because I care about your growth and want to support you."
- "I want us to think together about how we can make [situation] even better."
- "I'm curious about [situation]. Can we explore what's happening and what we might do differently?"

**Powerful opening questions:**
- "What's on your mind about [project/area]?"
- "If you could change one thing about how [situation] is going, what would it be?"
- "What's been challenging for you lately?"

**Coaching move for the planner:** "How are you planning to open? Is there a risk you'll start with your agenda before hearing theirs?"

---

## Exploration: Seeking Self-Realization

**Goal:** Help the person arrive at their own insight. Resist the urge to shortcut to the answer.

**The Coaching Habit questions (in order):**
1. "What's on your mind?" — Opens the conversation
2. "And what else?" — Deepens the exploration (use this multiple times)
3. "What's the real challenge here for you?" — Gets to the core issue
4. "What do you want?" — Clarifies their desired outcome
5. "How can I help?" — Defines your role

**Additional exploration questions:**
- "What's working well? What could work even better?"
- "If you were advising someone else in this situation, what would you tell them?"
- "What patterns are you noticing?"
- "What would success look like three months from now?"

**Coaching move:** "What questions will help them arrive at their own insights? How will you resist solving it for them?"

---

## When Self-Realization Doesn't Happen

If they don't see the issue after genuine exploration, the leader may need to share their perspective — but without abandoning the coaching stance.

**Steps:**
1. **Ask permission to share:** "I have some observations I'd like to share. Would that be helpful?"
2. **Provide specific, behavioral examples with curiosity:** "Here's what I've seen: [specific instance]. What's your take on that?"
3. **Name the impact and ask for their response:** "When [behavior happens], the impact is [consequence]. What are your thoughts on that?"
4. **Return to coaching questions:** "Given this feedback, what are you thinking now?"

**Coaching move:** "What specific examples will you use if you need to share your perspective? How will you do so while maintaining a coaching stance?"

---

## Creating the Path Forward

**Goal:** Co-create solutions. The employee owns the plan.

**Questions that create accountability:**
- "If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?" — Clarifies trade-offs
- "What will you do? When will you do it? How will I know?" — Creates accountability
- "What's one small step you could take this week?"
- "What support do you need from me?"
- "What obstacles might get in your way? How will you handle them?"

**Establish clear commitments:**
- "So I'm hearing you'll [specific action] by [specific date]. Did I get that right?"
- "Let's agree to check in on [date]. What would be most helpful to discuss then?"

**Coaching move:** "How will you ensure they own the solution while still being clear about expectations?"

---

## Social Contracting

**Goal:** After they've identified what they'll do differently, establish *how* the leader will support their success going forward.

**Ask them how they want to be supported:**
- "If I see you slipping back into [old pattern], how can I best redirect you?"
- "What's the most helpful way for me to give you a nudge if this starts happening again?"
- "How do you want me to raise it if I notice [behavior] coming back?"

**Co-create the agreement:**
- Listen to their preference (public vs. private, direct vs. signal, immediate vs. later)
- Confirm: "So you're saying if I see [X], you'd like me to [Y]. Is that right?"
- Add your own needs if necessary: "That works for me. And I also need [Z]. Can we agree to that?"

**Frame it as partnership:** "This is about setting both of us up for success."

**Coaching move:** "What specific redirection approach will work best for this person? How will you honor their preferences while still maintaining accountability?"

---

## Closing

**Goal:** Reinforce their ownership and leave them energized.

**Closing sequence:**
1. "What was most useful about this conversation for you?" *(Learning Question — always close with this)*
2. Recap their commitments (not yours — they own it)
3. Recap your agreed-upon way of redirecting if needed
4. "What's your key takeaway?"
5. "I'm here as a thinking partner whenever you need."

---

## Post-Conversation

**Follow-up actions:**
- Document the conversation and their commitments
- Document the agreed-upon redirection approach
- Schedule the follow-up check-in
- Provide any resources or support promised
- Observe and acknowledge progress: "I noticed [improvement]. What changed for you?"
- Use the agreed-upon redirection method if needed

**Coaching move:** "How will you continue coaching rather than managing in your follow-ups?"
