# The 7 Essential Coaching Questions — Viewrail Leadership Reference

*From Michael Bungay Stanier's The Coaching Habit*

The 7 questions form a complete coaching toolkit. Used consistently, they shift conversations from advice-giving to growth-building, helping teams solve problems, make better decisions, and learn faster.

Use this reference when a leader needs a quick refresher on the questions, a reminder of when and how to use each one, or a guide to build the habit of coaching in everyday conversations.

---

## Why These Questions Work

- They keep the **employee in the driver's seat** — building ownership, accountability, and problem-solving.
- They stop managers from defaulting to **giving advice or rescuing**.
- They build a **habit of curiosity**: short, simple, but powerful conversations that spark growth.

---

## The 7 Questions

**1. The Kickstart Question — "What's on your mind?"**
- Cuts through small talk and gets straight to what matters most.
- Opens the door to meaningful issues: priorities, challenges, opportunities.
- **Manager tip:** Use it to set the tone for 1:1s. Don't overcomplicate — just listen.

**2. The AWE Question — "And what else?"**
- Simple follow-up that often unlocks the *real* issue after the first answer.
- Shows genuine curiosity and prevents rushing to solutions.
- **Manager tip:** Ask 2–3 times. Most insight comes after the second "what else?"

**3. The Focus Question — "What's the real challenge here for you?"**
- Moves the conversation from vague problems to the heart of the matter.
- Shifts agency from external factors to the person.
- **Manager tip:** Emphasize the *"for you"* — this keeps responsibility centered on them.

**4. The Foundation Question — "What do you want?"**
- Surfaces clarity about motives and desired outcomes.
- Prevents time wasted on unclear or unspoken needs.
- **Manager tip:** If they hesitate, try: *"If you did know, what would it be?"*

**5. The Lazy Question — "How can I help?"**
- Stops the leader from jumping into Rescuer mode.
- Forces the employee to articulate exactly what support they want.
- **Manager tip:** If their ask isn't reasonable, you can negotiate — but at least it's clear.

**6. The Strategic Question — "If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?"**
- Highlights trade-offs, capacity, and priorities.
- Prevents overcommitment and helps employees make conscious choices.
- **Manager tip:** Use when someone is enthusiastic about new work but already stretched.

**7. The Learning Question — "What was most useful for you?"**
- Reinforces reflection, insight, and retention of the conversation.
- Gives the leader feedback about their coaching impact.
- **Manager tip:** End 1:1s or check-ins with this — it builds a culture of learning.

---

## Practical Coaching Reminders

- **Silence is powerful** — ask, then wait. Don't fill the gap.
- **Stay curious longer** — resist the urge to "solve" too quickly.
- **Use them flexibly** — not every conversation needs all seven. Pick the one that fits.
- **Build habits** — these questions work best when woven into everyday check-ins, not just formal reviews.

---

## Using the Questions as a Conversation Arc

The questions can be used in order as a natural coaching flow:

| Question | Purpose | When to Use |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| "What's on your mind?" | Open | Always start here |
| "And what else?" | Deepen | After every first answer |
| "What's the real challenge here for you?" | Focus | When the issue feels vague |
| "What do you want?" | Clarify | When direction is unclear |
| "How can I help?" | Define your role | Before jumping to advice |
| "If you say yes to this, what are you saying no to?" | Prioritize | When trade-offs matter |
| "What was most useful for you?" | Close | End every coaching conversation |
