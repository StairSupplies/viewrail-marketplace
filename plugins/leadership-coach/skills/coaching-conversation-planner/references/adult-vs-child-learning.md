# Adult vs. Child Learning — Viewrail Leadership Reference

*Viewrail leadership development content — why coaching must replace telling as the default*

Use this reference when a leader needs to understand the *why* behind coaching — especially when they're skeptical, keep defaulting to advice-giving, or feel like coaching takes too long compared to just telling people what to do.

---

## The Shift Leaders Must Make

In school, learning was teacher-driven — structured, directed, and focused on the "right answers." In the workplace, that model fails. Employees are adults. They don't need instruction — they need growth, ownership, and relevance. That's why **coaching must replace telling** as the default leadership approach.

---

## How Adults Learn at Work

- **Self-Directed:** Adults take ownership when they feel trusted and empowered. Coaching invites them to think, decide, and act.
- **Experience-Based:** Every employee brings valuable experiences. Coaching draws those out and builds on them instead of overriding them.
- **Relevance-Driven:** Adults learn best when the "why" connects to real work. Coaching keeps learning tied to goals, performance, and purpose.
- **Problem-Centered:** Real improvement happens through solving today's challenges, not through lectures. Coaching conversations focus on practical action.
- **Internally Motivated:** Recognition, mastery, and autonomy drive engagement more than external rewards. Coaching activates this internal motivation.

---

## What Doesn't Work: The Child Learning Model

Traditional management often mirrors child learning:

- **Dependent Learners:** Waiting for answers or approval.
- **Limited Autonomy:** Tasks are prescribed instead of explored.
- **Externally Motivated:** Driven by feedback, grades, or compliance.
- **Instructional Approach:** Managers teach or tell instead of ask and guide.

This model limits initiative, ownership, and innovation — the very things high-performing teams need.

---

## From Managing to Coaching

| Old Model (Pedagogical) | New Model (Coaching / Andragogical) |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Manager directs | Leader facilitates growth |
| Focus on control | Focus on empowerment |
| Task completion | Skill and mindset development |
| Motivation through compliance | Motivation through autonomy |
| Feedback as judgment | Feedback as curiosity and discovery |

---

## The Bottom Line

Coaching isn't just a leadership skill — it's the system that supports how adults actually learn. When leaders coach, they stop managing behavior and start developing capability.

**Coaching question to ask the skeptical leader:** "When was the last time someone *telling* you what to do actually changed how you thought about something? Compare that to a time when someone asked you a great question and you figured it out yourself. Which one stuck?"
