# Coaching and Adult Learning — Viewrail Leadership Reference

*Viewrail leadership development content — connects The Coaching Habit to how adults actually learn*

In school, learning was teacher-driven. We sat, listened, took notes, and repeated the "right" answers back. That model worked for children who needed structure and direction. But the workplace is different. Employees are adults — and adults don't learn by being told what to do. They learn by doing, reflecting, and connecting new insights to their own experiences.

That's why leaders must stop managing behavior and start coaching for growth.

Use this reference when preparing a leader for a coaching conversation or 1:1 — especially to help them understand *why* questions work better than answers, grounded in how adults actually learn.

---

## Why Coaching Works for Adults

Adults are **self-directed** learners who bring valuable experiences to the table. They're **internally motivated** — driven by autonomy, mastery, and purpose. They learn best when the "why" is clear and the learning connects to what matters most in their work.

Coaching honors this. It invites employees to think for themselves instead of waiting for direction. It transforms feedback from judgment into curiosity. It builds capability, not dependency.

**Coaching question for the leader:** "When was the last time someone telling you what to do actually changed how you thought about something? Compare that to a time when someone asked you a great question and you figured it out yourself. Which one stuck?"

---

## The 7 Questions That Bring Adult Learning to Life

Michael Bungay Stanier's *The Coaching Habit* offers a framework that aligns perfectly with how adults learn. Each question shifts ownership back to the employee and keeps the leader focused on curiosity instead of control.

### In Order — Use as a Coaching Conversation Arc:

**1. "What's on your mind?"** *(Kickstart)*
Gets straight to what's meaningful and relevant. Starts with their priorities — not yours.
*Adult learning principle: Relevance — learning begins with what matters to them.*

**2. "And what else?"** *(AWE Question)*
Encourages deeper thinking. Adults process through dialogue, and this question draws out reflection before jumping to solutions.
*Adult learning principle: Experience — their first answer is rarely their deepest one.*

**3. "What's the real challenge here for you?"** *(Focus)*
Moves beyond surface-level issues to uncover the root cause. Helps develop problem-solving muscles.
*Adult learning principle: Problem-centered learning — focus on the real obstacle.*

**4. "What do you want?"** *(Foundation)*
Builds clarity and accountability. Adults are motivated by clear goals they helped define.
*Adult learning principle: Autonomy — they own the direction.*

**5. "How can I help?"** *(Lazy)*
Forces specificity. Instead of rescuing, invites partnership and empowers the employee to articulate what they need.
*Adult learning principle: Self-direction — they define what support looks like.*

**6. "If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?"** *(Strategic)*
Helps them weigh trade-offs. A skill essential for self-management and prioritization.
*Adult learning principle: Real-world application — connects to actual constraints.*

**7. "What was most useful for you?"** *(Learning)*
Reinforces reflection and learning. Closes the loop. Helps adults internalize insights and feel ownership of their growth.
*Adult learning principle: Reflection — learning becomes ongoing and personal.*

---

## Five Adult Learning Principles — and How Each Question Activates Them

| Principle | What it means | Which question activates it |
|-----------|--------------|----------------------------|
| **Relevance** | Stays tied to real work and goals | "What's on your mind?" |
| **Experience** | Their perspectives become the foundation | "And what else?" |
| **Autonomy** | The leader facilitates instead of directs | "What do you want?" / "How can I help?" |
| **Problem-Centered** | Focus on practical challenges, not theory | "What's the real challenge here for you?" |
| **Reflection** | Learning becomes ongoing and personal | "What was most useful for you?" |

## The Bottom Line for Viewrail Leaders

The best leaders aren't teachers — they're coaches. They don't provide answers; they create space for discovery. Coaching transforms "managers who tell" into "leaders who grow."

When leaders coach in a way that matches how adults actually learn, performance, engagement, and ownership all rise together.

**The shift isn't about saying less.** It's about asking better.

---

## Coaching Moves for the Conversation Planner Session

When helping a leader prepare their coaching questions, use this reference to:

1. **Check their questions against the 7.** Are they starting with "What's on your mind?" or jumping straight to their agenda?
2. **Test for the AWE habit.** "In your last 1:1, how many times did you ask 'And what else?' before moving on?"
3. **Close the planning session with the Learning Question.** Model it yourself: "What was most useful for you in this prep conversation?"
4. **Connect to ownership.** "If your team member could only own one thing coming out of this conversation, what would it be? How will your questions lead them there?"
