# Leading with Trust — Viewrail Leadership Reference

At Viewrail, we talk often about *Better Together* — the idea that collaboration, transparency, and follow-through drive performance. But underneath every great team, there's one force that determines whether things move fast or stall completely: **trust**.

When trust is low, even simple decisions feel hard. People hold back, over-check, or avoid accountability. Projects slow down, communication gets tense, and stress builds. When trust is high, everything changes. Information flows. Expectations are clear. People take ownership.

The same people, the same talent — completely different outcomes. That's the power of trust.

Use this reference when coaching any leader on **Builds Trust & Psychological Safety** or when trust has surfaced as the underlying issue in a conversation, team dynamic, or reflection.

---

## The Speed of Trust — Stephen M.R. Covey

Covey's formula is simple:

**Low Trust = Low Speed + High Cost**
**High Trust = High Speed + Low Cost**

When trust is missing, we pay a "trust tax." Projects get delayed, teams over-manage, and innovation stalls. When trust is strong, we earn a "trust dividend." Decisions happen faster, handoffs are smoother, and results compound.

For leaders, trust isn't a soft skill — it's a measurable performance variable.

**Coaching question:** "Where in your team or projects do you see the trust tax showing up — places where low trust is slowing things down or increasing effort?"

---

## The 4 Cores of Credibility

People don't trust titles — they trust *credibility*. Credibility has four parts:

1. **Integrity** — Do what you say. Admit mistakes fast.
2. **Intent** — Let people see your motives. Explain the "why" behind decisions.
3. **Capabilities** — Stay sharp. Keep learning and asking for feedback.
4. **Results** — Deliver consistently. Follow through, visibly.

Strong credibility earns confidence. Weak credibility erodes it quietly, one missed commitment at a time.

**Coaching question:** "Which of these four cores, if strengthened, would most change how others see your reliability as a leader?"

---

## 3 Behaviors That Build Trust Fast

While trust takes time to sustain, it doesn't have to take time to start:

### 1. Talk Straight
Say what you mean — early and clearly.
*"Here's what I know. Here's what I don't. Here's what happens next."*
Clarity builds confidence. Spin and avoidance destroy it.

### 2. Clarify Expectations
Most breakdowns aren't about bad intent — they're about unclear expectations.
Before starting any project or conversation, ask:
- What does success look like?
- Who owns what?
- By when?
- How will we measure "done"?

### 3. Keep Commitments
The fastest way to build trust is to *do what you say you'll do*.
And when you can't — renegotiate early.
Trust grows through consistency, not grand gestures.

**Coaching question:** "Of these three behaviors, which one would make the biggest difference on your team if practiced more consistently?"

---

## Smart Trust

Leading with trust doesn't mean blind optimism. **Smart Trust** means giving trust deliberately — with clear boundaries and accountability.

Ask leaders:
- Where in your team or projects do you sense a trust gap?
- Which behavior — Talk Straight, Clarify Expectations, or Keep Commitments — would help close it?
- What one action will you take this week to rebuild confidence?

---

## Why It Matters at Viewrail

Trust isn't a nice-to-have. It's the multiplier behind everything we do. When we lead with trust, we move faster, think deeper, and work *Better Together*.

Every time a leader talks straight, clarifies expectations, or keeps a commitment, they don't just get a task done — they make the next one easier.

Leadership at Viewrail isn't about control. It's about credibility. And credibility is built one kept promise at a time.

## Reflection Questions to Use in Coaching

These are Viewrail's own questions for leaders working on trust — use them in Reflection Journal sessions or after a trust-related coaching conversation:

1. "Where in your work do you see the trust tax showing up — places where low trust slows things down or increases effort?"
2. "Which of the four cores of credibility — integrity, intent, capabilities, or results — do you most want to strengthen? Why?"
3. "Of the three behaviors that build trust fast, which one would make the biggest difference on your team if practiced more consistently?"
4. "What's one action you can take this week to earn a trust dividend within your team or partnerships?"

## Connection to Driver 3: Builds Trust & Psychological Safety

Use this reference alongside the *Crucial Conversations* framework. Where Crucial Conversations provides the *conversation skills* (Start with Heart, Make It Safe, State Your Path), the Speed of Trust framework provides the *leadership credibility* that makes those skills land. You can have a perfectly structured conversation and still fail to build trust if your credibility is low.

The bridge: **Credibility earns you the right to have crucial conversations.** Without it, even well-crafted honesty lands as attack.
