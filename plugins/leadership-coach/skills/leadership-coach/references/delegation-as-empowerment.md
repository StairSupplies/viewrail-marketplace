# Delegation as Empowerment — Viewrail Leadership Reference

*Viewrail leadership development content — a five-step framework for empowering through delegation*

Delegation done right isn't about offloading work — it's about multiplying impact. This framework gives leaders a practical system for deciding what to delegate, to whom, and how to support without rescuing.

Use this reference when coaching a leader who is over-functioning, holding their team back, or struggling to trust their people with meaningful work.

---

## Step 1: Identify Work Types — The Delegation Matrix

| Task Type | Delegate? | Why |
|-----------|-----------|-----|
| **Low Value / Low Skill** (routine, admin, recurring) | ✅ Yes | Builds efficiency, frees leader's time |
| **Low Value / High Skill** (report formatting, scheduling) | ✅ Yes | Great development opportunities, not the best use of leader time |
| **High Value / Low Skill** (stretch projects, presentations, problem-solving) | ✅ Yes (with support) | Develops employee capability and ownership |
| **High Value / High Skill** (strategy, vision, sensitive issues) | ❌ No | Requires leader expertise and authority |

---

## Step 2: Apply the 70% Rule

- If someone can do the work **70% as well as you**, delegate it. The remaining 30% will develop with practice.
- Don't hold back because "they won't do it exactly like me." Different doesn't mean wrong.
- Ask yourself: *If I invest time coaching them now, how much time will this save me and grow them later?*

---

## Step 3: Match People to Opportunities

- **Readiness:** Consider the employee's current competence. Delegate simple tasks to build confidence, then graduate to stretch projects.
- **Motivation:** Align tasks with career goals and interests. Delegation feels like empowerment when it connects to personal growth.
- **Bandwidth:** Ensure they have the time and resources. Overloading someone creates frustration, not development.
- **Fairness:** Rotate opportunities so delegation isn't seen as favoritism or as dumping.

---

## Step 4: Transfer Ownership Clearly

- **Define the outcome:** What does success look like? Be specific (deliverable, quality, timing).
- **Clarify boundaries:** What decisions are theirs vs. what requires your sign-off? What resources are available?
- **Delegate progressively:** For bigger projects, start by assigning smaller components, then expand responsibility as capability grows.
- **Agree on check-ins:** Establish touchpoints (e.g., quick weekly sync) instead of hovering. This builds trust and accountability.
- **Document if needed:** For bigger projects, a simple written brief helps ensure shared understanding.

---

## Step 5: Coach & Recognize

- **Support without rescuing:** Resist the urge to step in too early. Ask guiding questions instead.
- **Normalize mistakes:** Treat errors as part of learning. Debrief what went wrong and how to improve.
- **Recognize progress:** Call out initiative, ownership, and growth — not just final results.
- **Link to the bigger picture:** Show how their work contributes to team and company success. This creates meaning and pride.

---

## Leader's Mindset

Delegation isn't about reducing your workload — it's about **multiplying impact**:

- **Trust:** Believing in your people builds confidence.
- **Growth:** Delegation is the most practical form of development.
- **Capacity:** Every task you hand off creates room for more strategic leadership work.
- **Ownership:** Employees who own outcomes feel invested in the success of the business.
- **Legacy:** A leader's real impact is not in what they accomplish personally, but in how many others they elevate.
