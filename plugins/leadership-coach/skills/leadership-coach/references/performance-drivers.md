# Viewrail Leadership Performance Drivers

These six drivers define what great leadership looks like at Viewrail. Weave them naturally and explicitly into every coaching interaction. Reference them by name, connect behaviors to them, and use the maturity scales to orient feedback.

**Full reference:** https://sites.google.com/viewrail.com/viewrail/performance-drivers/leadership

For deep coaching on any driver, load `references/source-books.md` for the foundational frameworks, key concepts, and coaching language from the books behind each driver.

---

## 1. Develops People Through Coaching
**Foundational Book: *The Coaching Habit* — Michael Bungay Stanier**

A great leader's primary role is to help people grow, not to solve every problem for them. This means asking powerful, open-ended questions that spark self-discovery, build ownership, and strengthen confidence. It's about resisting the "Advice Monster" and creating space for others to think, reflect, and act. Great leaders share their own experiences without prescribing solutions, listen deeply, challenge assumptions, and help team members clarify what they want and how to get there.

**Key concepts from the book:** The Advice Monster, the AWE Question ("And what else?"), the 7 Essential Questions, building a coaching habit one conversation at a time.

**Maturity Scale:**
- *Beginner* — tends to provide answers instead of coaching; team waits for the leader to solve problems
- *Advanced* — recognized as a coaching leader who develops leaders who coach others

## 2. Leads With an Outward Mindset
**Foundational Book: *Leadership and Self-Deception* — The Arbinger Institute**

Leading with an outward mindset means seeing others as people with needs, goals, and pressures as real as your own — and acting accordingly. This shift moves a leader from self-focus to genuine curiosity about others' perspectives, fostering empathy and connection. Leaders with an outward mindset take responsibility for their impact, consider the broader needs of the business, and ensure decisions serve more than just their own priorities.

**Key concepts from the book:** In the Box vs. Out of the Box, self-betrayal, collusion, the shift from seeing people as obstacles or tools to seeing them as full human beings.

**Maturity Scale:**
- *Beginner* — focuses mostly on own tasks and goals, interactions feel transactional; tends to be "in the box"
- *Advanced* — seamlessly anticipates and balances the needs of people, team, and business; naturally outward

## 3. Builds Trust & Psychological Safety
**Foundational Book: *Crucial Conversations* — Patterson, Grenny, McMillan & Switzler**

Trust and psychological safety are the invisible infrastructure of high-performing teams. Leaders build them by acting with integrity, delivering on commitments, and inviting candid dialogue without fear of punishment. They admit mistakes openly, encourage dissenting views, use inclusive language, and create an environment where failure is seen as a valuable opportunity for growth.

**Key concepts from the book:** Start with Heart, Make It Safe (mutual purpose + mutual respect), the silence-to-violence spectrum, separating facts from stories, the Pool of Shared Meaning.

**Maturity Scale:**
- *Beginner* — keeps communication surface-level, team members hesitate to speak openly; avoids or forces crucial conversations
- *Advanced* — creates deep psychological safety where trust is a cultural norm and team members always feel they can be 100% honest

## 4. Empowers Through Ownership
**Foundational Book: *Turn the Ship Around!* — L. David Marquet**

Empowering through ownership means pushing decision-making down to the team and giving them real control over their work. Instead of dictating actions, leaders encourage team members to state their intentions — forcing thoughtful consideration and accountability before execution. This fosters commitment over compliance, surfaces skill gaps, and drives clarity around goals.

**Key concepts from the book:** Leader-Leader vs. Leader-Follower, "I intend to..." (stating intentions instead of asking permission), control + competence + clarity, resisting the pull to give solutions.

**Maturity Scale:**
- *Beginner* — directs tasks closely, team waits for instructions; operating in a Leader-Follower model
- *Advanced* — ownership and intent are part of the culture, team members act like leaders; true Leader-Leader model

## 5. Drives Clarity & Purpose
**Foundational Book: *The Advantage* — Patrick Lencioni**

Clarity and purpose are the antidotes to distraction and wasted effort. Leaders who excel here connect the team's work to a meaningful "Why" while focusing attention on the single most important priority right now. They articulate both the long-term vision and the immediate constraint that must be addressed, and link priorities to company-level strategy through Hoshin Kanri.

**Key concepts from the book:** The Six Critical Questions, the Rallying Cry (single most important priority), over-communicating clarity, purpose before goals, organizational health through alignment.

**Maturity Scale:**
- *Beginner* — team is often unclear on priorities, explains "what" but not "why"; no rallying cry
- *Advanced* — creates enduring clarity and purpose that aligns team, department, and organization; team can answer all six questions

---

## 6. Practices Courageous Stewardship
**Foundational Book: *The Servant Leader* — Ken Blanchard**

Courageous stewardship is the discipline of protecting people, purpose, and long-term health — especially when short-term pressures tempt compromise. These leaders act as caretakers of the culture, making decisions that align with values even when unpopular. They shield their teams from unnecessary churn, model integrity, and ensure success is measured by how people are treated as much as by results achieved.

**Key concepts from the book:** Leadership as stewardship (holding people and mission in trust), vision-first servant leadership, ego vs. service, the SERVE model, making courageous decisions that protect long-term health over short-term comfort.

**Maturity Scale:**
- *Beginner* — prioritizes short-term wins, may ignore values under pressure; leading for ego rather than service
- *Advanced* — serves as a cultural steward making bold, values-based decisions that safeguard the long-term health of people and organization
